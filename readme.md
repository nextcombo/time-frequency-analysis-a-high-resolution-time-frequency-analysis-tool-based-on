# Time-frequency Analysis: A High-Resolution Time-Frequency Analysis Tool Based on Multi-Synchronous Squeezing Transformation for Non-Stationary Signal Analysis [with MATLAB Code]

This paper proposes a high-resolution time-frequency (TF) analysis method for the analysis of strongly non-stationary signals. The time-frequency representations generated by traditional methods are often too vague to provide accurate features for such signals. The Multi-Sequential Synchronous Squeezing Transform (MSST) method, introduced in recent years, overcomes most of the problems present in traditional methods and is a promising tool. However, MSST still faces a major issue, the "unreallocated point problem," which can lead to the blurring of energy at certain special time-frequency points. This paper primarily focuses on addressing this issue. It was found that the problem in MSST is caused by rounding operations during the redistribution step in MSST. Subsequently, a simple strategy was proposed to effectively solve this problem, along with a discrete implementation scheme. Numerical analysis shows that compared to MSST, the proposed method can effectively improve energy concentration. A comparison with other advanced methods also demonstrates that the proposed method performs better in processing strongly non-stationary signals and noisy signals. In experimental signal analysis, three experiments were conducted to verify the effectiveness of the proposed method in actual signal analysis.
Introduction Rotating machinery with bearing defects typically generates pulse signals, which can be recorded by vibration sensors [1-7]. However, when the machine is running at different speeds, vibration signal processing becomes extremely challenging. This is because in this case, the measured signals usually exhibit strong non-stationarity characteristics, i.e. The signal frequency changes dramatically with time. Non-stationary signal analysis has received increasing attention in the field of fault diagnosis for rotating machinery. Time-frequency analysis (TFA) technology has a strong capability to deal with such challenges. Multiple articles have reviewed the development of TFA technology in rotating machinery fault diagnosis [8-10]. Recent studies have shown that energy concentration is a key indicator for evaluating the performance of TFA methods, as concentrated time-frequency representations (TFR) can better characterize potential fault features in signals. However, due to the limitations of Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle, traditional TFA methods (such as Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT), Wavelet Transform (WT) and S-transform) are difficult to provide concentrated results when dealing with strong time-varying signals. In recent decades, various novel TFA techniques aimed at overcoming the shortcomings of traditional methods have attracted widespread attention. These include redistribution method (RM) [12], synchronous shear transformation (SST) [13,14], demodulation synchronous shear transformation (DSST) [15,16], high-order synchronous shear transformation [17-19], synchronous extraction transformation (SET) [20,21], and multi-synchronous shear transformation (MSST) [22].
Reweighting Method (RM) maps data to new coordinates closer to the energy concentration area by locally estimating instantaneous frequency (IF) and group delay, sharpening the time-frequency representation of energy smearing. This time-frequency representation is typically generated by STFT, WT, or S transforms. However, the mapping applied by RM involves mapping to spectral or scaled graphs (usually defined as the square amplitude of STFT or WT), which loses the phase information of the signal, meaning that it is not possible to reconstruct the signal from the results of RM. As a post-processing technique similar to RM, SST performs time-frequency representation mapping based solely on IF estimation, preserving the ability to inversely reconstruct the signal, making it more advantageous in areas such as mechanical fault diagnosis [23,24], seismic signal analysis [25,26], and respiratory dynamics analysis [27]. Recent studies have focused on further enhancing the capabilities of SST in representing strong time-varying signals [13-22].
SST technology is proposed within the framework of linear TFA algorithms (such as WT, STFT, and S transform). However, constrained by the limitations of linear time-frequency atoms, linear TFA methods cannot effectively handle strong time-varying signals, resulting in energy smearing in corresponding time-frequency results, and SST also faces the same issue. Demodulation techniques design nonlinear time-frequency atoms to characterize time-varying signals, effectively overcoming the problems of linear TFA methods. Moreover, novel SST methods based on demodulation techniques show good potential in enhancing energy concentration, often referred to as DSST methods [15,16]. However, demodulation techniques must be designed based on prior information about the signal to design nonlinear time-frequency atoms [9]. In practice, determining the key information about the true signal ahead of time is often very difficult, if not impossible, hindering the engineering application of DSST techniques.
High-order SST methods are used to process high-frequency modulation (FM) signals without the need for prior signal information [17-19]. Theoretically, SST assumes that the analyzed signal should be purely harmonic, meaning SST is only suitable for weak FM signals. To improve this situation, a higher-order SST framework is built on more complex signal models, such as linear FM signals and higher-order polynomial FM signals. In the absence of noise, higher-order SST can provide highly concentrated results for strong time-varying signals. However, recent studies have found that higher-order SST methods are very sensitive to noise and struggle to achieve satisfactory results when processing strong noise signals [22].
SET methods aim to retain only the time-frequency coefficients closely related to the signal's time-varying characteristics and remove most of the weakly correlated time-frequency coefficients, hence resulting in SET results being more concentrated than SST results. However, SET can only provide an approximate reconstruction of the signal, and its reconstruction performance decreases with increasing nonlinearity of the signal.
The MSST method was proposed in recent papers, which improves the energy concentration of SST through an iterative process. MSST allows for perfect reconstruction of the signal and does not require prior information. Studies in the literature [22] show that MSST can provide more concentrated results than weighted distribution methods, DST, and higher-order SST when processing both noisy and noisy signals, seemingly making it a powerful tool for achieving ideal time-frequency representation (ITFR). However, the discussion section in the literature [22] points out that there is a major issue with MSST, namely "unreassigned points problem," which hinders the concentrated representation of time-frequency features.
This paper first provides a detailed theoretical analysis of the unreallocated point issue in MSST, and then proposes an improved MSST method (IMSST) to effectively solve this problem. This new method still allows for perfect signal reconstruction and has good robustness against noise, without the need for additional parameters or prior information on the signal, with limited computational cost. The structure of this paper is as follows: Section 2 elaborates on the theoretical basis of the proposed method; Sections 3 and 4 provide numerical verification and experimental validation, respectively; Section 5 concludes the paper.
Conclusion This paper first investigates the causes of the unreallocated point issue in MSST methods, then proposes an effective solution, ultimately establishing a highly concentrated time-frequency analysis method called the Improved Multi-Synchronous Squeezing Transform (IMSST). The proposed method can better handle various time-varying signals, even when the signals contain strong noise. Through comparison with other technologies, the advantages of the proposed method are verified using both numerical signals and experimental signals. Verification results show that by leveraging concentrated time-frequency features, complex time-varying signals and potential physical phenomena can be identified.
